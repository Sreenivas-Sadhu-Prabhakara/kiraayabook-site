01 · The problem

You own the flats.
The chasing owns your month.

If you run four flats in Pune or twelve PG beds in Kondapur, nobody handed you an accounts department. Your system is a WhatsApp thread, a physical diary, and your own memory — and all three quietly break down.

💬

WhatsApp forgets, so you don't get to

Reminders get typed one by one, some tenants get chased twice, some slip through. By the 10th you're scrolling to reconstruct who actually paid.

📓

The diary is one spill away from gone

Arrears live in a ledger only you can read. Carrying a Rs 9,500 balance forward for two months is a note in the margin — until it isn't.

🔌

The electricity split is a monthly argument

New meter reading, old reading, per-unit rate, who used what. It's ten minutes of maths and a screenshot, every single tenant.

🧾

"Bhaiya, receipt bhej dena"

Paid tenants want proof. You're photographing a signed slip or promising a receipt you'll send later — and later never quite comes.

02 · The insight

Rent is the most predictable recurring payment there is — same tenants, same amount, same day — yet it's the one still chased by hand, every month, on WhatsApp.

KiraayaBook's whole job is to close that gap: take a thing that repeats perfectly and let it run as a cycle — generate the dues, send the reminders, issue the receipt — so collection happens whether or not you remembered to start it.

03 · How it works

Set it up once. Then every month runs itself.

  1. 1

    Add your units and leases

    Enter each flat or PG bed, the tenant, the rent, and the due day. That's the whole setup — takes an evening for a full building.

  2. 2

    On the due day, invoices generate

    KiraayaBook creates one invoice per active lease and rolls any carried-forward arrears into each tenant's balance. No button to press.

  3. 3

    Staged reminders go out on WhatsApp

    A nudge 3 days before, a note on the due day, a firmer one if it goes overdue — each drafted to your tenant, ready in the outbox.

  4. 4

    Paid marks itself, receipt goes back

    When a tenant pays, the due clears and a digital receipt is issued automatically. Your dashboard always shows collected vs pending.

04 · What's inside

The month's whole rent engine, in one book.

Monthly rent cycle

One invoice per active lease, raised automatically on its due day. Set the day once; it repeats forever.

Dues & arrears roll-up

Every tenant carries a running balance — this month plus anything unpaid before it. No margin notes to reconcile.

Staged WhatsApp reminders

T-3 days, due day, and overdue nudges written to your outbox — polite, personal, and never sent twice.

Instant digital receipts

The moment a payment lands, the tenant gets a clean receipt with the flat, month and amount. Proof, handled.

Electricity split by meter

Punch in the new reading; KiraayaBook computes each tenant's units and adds the charge to their invoice. No manual maths.

Vacancy flag on empty units

An empty flat is marked vacant, drops out of the billing run, and shows as lost income you can see at a glance.

07 · Who it's for & questions

Built for the small landlord, not the property empire.

KiraayaBook is for the owner of a handful of units — four flats, a single building, a 15-bed PG. Big enough that chasing rent eats your evenings; small enough that a property-management suite is overkill and overpriced. If you're currently running it on WhatsApp and a diary, this is for you.

Individual landlords PG & hostel owners Family-owned buildings 2–30 units
Does it actually send messages, or just remind me to?

KiraayaBook drafts each reminder — personalised, with the tenant's name, month and balance — and stages it in your outbox on schedule. You send from your own number with a tap, so it always looks like it came from you, not a robot.

My tenants pay by cash and UPI. Does that work?

Yes. Mark a cash payment as received and the due clears and a receipt goes out, same as UPI. KiraayaBook tracks the balance — it doesn't force a single payment rail on your tenants.

I'm not very techy. How long to set up?

An evening. You add each unit, tenant, rent and due day once. After that the monthly cycle runs on its own — the whole point is that you stop doing the setup work every month.
